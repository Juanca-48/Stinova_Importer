STRINOVA TOON SETUP - USER GUIDE
================================

Blender add-on that imports Strinova characters and gets them ready to use:
toon shading, lighting, a rig with face controls, and hair and clothes physics.

Works with both model formats: .uemodel and .fbx.

Language: the panel is in English, or in Spanish when Blender's interface is
in Spanish (Edit > Preferences > Interface > Translation). What the add-on
creates inside the scene (bone collections, controls, collections) has English
names. Scenes made with versions before 1.1.1, which used Spanish names, are
renamed automatically when opened.
Spanish guide: INSTRUCCIONES.txt.


1. REQUIREMENTS
---------------

- Blender 5.2 or 5.3.
- Gacha Setup (the "setup_wizard" add-on): required to generate the rig.
  https://github.com/PaoloESAN/gacha-setup/releases
- UEFormat v10 or later: only to import .uemodel files.
  Not needed for .fbx.
  https://github.com/h4lfheart/UEFormat
- For the toon shading, the textures (.png) with the game's file names,
  next to the model or in a folder above it (for example FModel's export,
  ...\Kanami\ with its skins inside). The add-on finds them by itself.
  Each material's data, characters and weapons (which texture goes in each
  channel, shine, transparency...), is already bundled with the add-on
  (strinova_toon\materials.json), so FModel's .json files are not needed.
  If the folder does have the .json files, those are used (in case they are
  newer).
  The model can come from another source; without the textures the importer,
  the rig and the face work the same, but the materials stay as imported.


2. INSTALLATION
---------------

Normal installation:
  1. On GitHub, in the Releases section (right column of the repository),
     download strinova_toon-vX.Y.Z.zip from the latest version.
     Don't unzip it.
  2. (For the development version, without a release: green "Code >
     Download ZIP" button, unzip it and zip the "strinova_toon" folder on
     its own.)
  3. In Blender: Edit > Preferences > Add-ons > Install from Disk, pick the zip.
  4. Enable "Strinova Toon Setup" and "Gacha Setup".

For development (Blender loads the add-on straight from the repository):
  Close Blender and run in PowerShell:

    New-Item -ItemType Junction `
      -Path "$env:APPDATA\Blender Foundation\Blender\5.2\scripts\addons\strinova_toon" `
      -Target "<repository path>\strinova_toon"

  Then enable it in Preferences > Add-ons. Changes show up when you restart
  Blender or reload the add-on.


3. IMPORTING A CHARACTER
------------------------

The panel is in the 3D viewport, "Strinova" tab (N key).

- "Import character" accepts:
    * a .uemodel or .fbx file, or
    * a skin's folder: go into it without picking a file and press Import.
      It takes its 3D model (the lobby one if there is one, since it has the
      expressions) and its paper version, and imports them together.
- Checkboxes under the button:
    * Apply Toon Shading: materials, lighting and outline.
    * Generate rig: rig with Gacha Setup, face controls and physics.
      Under it, the IK method for arms and legs: Rotation or Poles (see 6).
    * Include paper version: also brings the skin's paper version.
- "Root folder": where the textures (and FModel's .json files) are
  taken from. It fills itself in on import, you don't need to set it first:
    * from FModel's export, it is the character's folder (...\Characters\<Name>);
    * anywhere else (an .fbx, another export), the add-on looks for the
      model's textures in its own folder first and then in the folders above
      it (up to 7 levels), and keeps the nearest one that has them.
  Only if they are nowhere near the model does it look for the character in
  FModel's export (the "FModel export folder" of the add-on's preferences)
  or use the folder already in the panel: set it to the folder with the
  textures and import again.
  The search never walks a whole drive or very large folders (your user
  folder, for example), so keep the textures near the model.
- Everything imported goes into a "<Character> <Skin>" collection, for
  example "Flavia S001" or "Kanami Ruiseñor" (taken from the model's folders
  .../<Character>/<Skin>/Mesh3D/...; a loose file uses its own name).

Which model to use: each skin has the match model (Mesh3D) and the lobby one
(Mesh3DLobby). Almost always only the lobby one has the facial expressions.


4. CHARACTER PANEL
------------------

With the character (mesh or rig) selected, the panel shows a collapsible
section with its name:

- Outline width: thickness of the outline.
- Paper version: switches between the 3D model and the paper one (see 8).
- Show <accessory>: shows or hides each accessory (see 9).
- Physics: turns physics on or off instantly. Animatable.
- Hair / Bangs / Clothes / Chest inertia: how much they lag and drag.
  Starting values: 0.56 / 0.28 / 0.32 / 0.20. Bangs and the front locks
  move half as much as the back hair.

These values are properties of each rig: every character has its own.
They are also in the rig's Object Properties > Custom Properties.


5. SHADING AND LIGHTING
-----------------------

- Materials are rebuilt from the textures and the material data (bundled
  with the add-on, or FModel's .json files if present): toon with sunlight,
  face with soft shadow (SDF), normal map and matcap.
- Outline: inverted hull ("ST Outline" modifier). No outline on the face and
  eyes, like in the game. Color: the base texture, darkened.
- Lighting controls (rig bone collection "Lighting"):
    * Wheels "Light" (the lit side) and "Ambient" (the shadow): the picker's
      position sets hue and saturation; the bar, brightness.
    * "ST Sun": light comes from this control toward the head and moves
      the shadows of the body and the face.
  They animate in the same action as the character.
- Each character has its own sun and controls: two characters can be lit
  differently (one indoors and one outdoors).
- The three head empties (ST_HeadOrigin / Forward / Up) give the head's
  orientation for the face shadow. They are disabled in the viewport to stay
  out of the way; turn them on with the monitor icon in the Outliner.


6. RIG AND PHYSICS
------------------

- Rigify rig generated with Gacha Setup's NTE rigger (Strinova shares its
  Bip001 skeleton).
- Constraint-based physics, like Gacha Setup: each hair, clothes and chest
  bone points at its child with a Damped Track and trails behind the body
  when animating.
- Limitation: the result depends on the frames evaluated before. Playing
  straight through doesn't give the same as jumping to a frame, and a render
  of single frames can differ from the viewport.
- Head follow: the head aims at the "head-controller" control in front of
  the face. The control follows the neck: turning the neck or the torso
  carries the head, and moving the control aims it. Its parent can be
  changed in the rig's panel (parent_switch). Turn it off with "Use Head
  Controller" on the gear above the head (plate-settings).
- Elbows and knees: chosen in the panel before importing, under "Generate
  rig" (same for arms and legs):
    * Rotation: rotate the IK control (upper_arm_ik, thigh_ik) to swing the
      elbow or knee. The poles are ready but off: the limb's "Pole" switch
      (Rigify panel) turns one on.
    * Poles: the elbow aims back and the knee forward at pole targets 60 cm
      away, shown in the "IK Poles" bone collection. The IK controls whose
      rotation swings them (upper_arm_ik, thigh_ik) do nothing then, so they
      are hidden (in "Other").
  Either way the knee bends forward. With Rotation the pole targets are
  hidden: show "IK Poles" (eye icon in the Bone Collections list) to move
  them.
- Hidden helpers: the limbs' settings bones (upper_arm_parent,
  thigh_parent) and the automatic shoulder's helpers are in the hidden
  "Other" collection; their settings still show in the panel.
- Gacha Setup's automatic shoulder is off: it twisted the upper arm when
  moving the hand. Turn it back on with "Toggle Shoulder Constraints" in
  plate-settings.


7. FACE
-------

Expression controls (shape keys) are sorted into titled sections, each with
its bone collection inside "Face":

  Left of the face: Eyes, Pupils, Brows.
  Right: Mouth, Lips, Head & Other.

- Mouth: the ones that open the mouth, and the phonemes. Lips: the ones
  that move it without opening it (smiles, corners, teeth, tongue). They are
  told apart by measuring the model's geometry when the rig is generated.
- Eyes, Pupils and Brows are split into "Both", "Right" and "Left".
  Facing the character, each block sits on the side
  of the face it moves.
- Each control is a small bar that slides up (0 at the bottom, 1 at the top)
  along a guide marking the minimum and maximum.
- Blink: if the model closes each eye with its own control, "CTRL-ST
  Blink" is added to the "Both" block of Eyes, closing both at once. Winks
  still work.
- Gaze: "Gaze" control in front of the face, with a target per eye
  (Gaze.L / Gaze.R).
- Face bones: Fragrans, Yugiri and Mara (and the eyelids of Chiyo's match
  model) have bones in the face. They show as circles in the "Face: Bones"
  collection. Teeth and tongue go in "Face: Inner Mouth", hidden on import.


8. PAPER VERSION
----------------

Each skin has a paper version (Mesh2D folder): a flat sheet with the
character painted on it.

- With "Include paper version" (or importing the skin's folder) it is
  imported along with the 3D model. It can also be imported later: if the
  rigged 3D character is already in the scene, it joins it.
- The same rig moves it and it uses the same lighting.
- The panel's "Paper version" button (the rig's st_paper property) switches
  between the two in a single frame. With a keyframe on each state the swap
  is instant. The game hides it with the animation (a spin, opening the
  arms): it's best to swap in the middle of a movement.
- Without its 3D character in the scene, the paper version is imported on
  its own, without a rig.


9. ACCESSORIES (MASKS, HATS)
----------------------------

Each accessory has a bone in the "Accessories" collection (a
small mask drawn in front of the piece). It's a child of the head: it follows
the character, but can be moved, rotated or scaled to 0. It also has a
"Show <accessory>" switch in the panel that hides it, with its outline, in
the viewport and the render.

- Masks with their own bone (Fragrans, Kanami S211-S215, MoBai S206-S210,
  Eika S111): the control is created automatically.
- Eika's masks (S001, S101, S103, S106, S107...): the "ACC-Mask" control
  is created automatically.
- Other accessories (most hats) share the head's or the hair's weights and
  have no bone of their own: they move with them.


10. WEAPONS
-----------

Weapons are imported separately, with the "Import weapon" button in the
panel's "Weapons" section. Importing a character never brings its weapon.

- Each weapon skin is several pieces: the body, its sight and sometimes a
  pendant, each also in a lobby version.
- Pick the weapon's body (or the skin's folder, without picking a file) and
  it comes in assembled: the sight and the pendant hang from their bones
  (Sight, Pendant) and follow the weapon. If the skin has no sight of its
  own, it uses the base weapon's.
- In the file browser's options, "Include sight" decides whether the sight
  comes in or only the body.
- If you pick any other piece (a sight, a pendant, a loose lobby version),
  only that piece is imported.
- Accepts .uemodel and .fbx. Each weapon goes into its own "Weapon <name>"
  collection and doesn't touch the characters in the scene.
- With "Apply Toon Shading" checked, the weapon gets the add-on's toon: the
  same light and shadow as the characters, a matcap shine where the weapon
  is polished metal, and its outline. Sight lenses become translucent glass.
- Lighting: if a character is selected when importing (or a weapon already
  using its light), the weapon uses that character's sun and panel colors,
  so it matches when you put it in their hand. Otherwise it creates its own
  sun.
- Textures are looked up in FModel's SkinAssets folder: next to the model
  if it comes from FModel. For a weapon kept elsewhere (an .fbx), like a
  character: in its own folder and the folders above it (the nearest one
  holding its textures); if they aren't there, in the
  "FModel export folder" of the add-on's preferences (Edit > Preferences >
  Add-ons > Strinova Toon Setup). It fills itself in the first time you
  import anything from FModel's export; you can also set it by hand. If you export missing textures with FModel while Blender is open,
  importing the weapon again finds them (no need to restart Blender).
- Weapon controls ("Weapon" bone collection):
    * Yellow frame (Root): moves and rotates the whole weapon.
    * Light blue circles: the moving parts, like the magazine (Magazine),
      bolt (Pull), suppressor (Suppressor), foregrip (Foregrip) or the
      katana's blade.
    * Pink circles: the sight and the pendant.
  The game's reference points (muzzle, shell ejection, aiming camera...)
  are in the hidden "Sockets" collection.
- Connect weapon: select the weapon and press "Connect weapon" in the
  Weapons section. The cursor turns into an eyedropper: click the character
  in the viewport (Esc or right click cancels; you can move the view
  meanwhile). The weapon goes to their right hand and also takes that
  character's light. Once connected, the panel shows who it's connected to,
  the controls for both hands, and "Disconnect weapon", which releases it
  and puts it back where it was before connecting.
  The "Right hand (trigger)" switch lets go of it or takes it again
  (animatable). Connected, the weapon follows the right hand, with the grip
  in the palm, the barrel along the fingers and the top toward the thumb.
  Off, the weapon goes back to where it was.
  If the right arm was at rest, connecting puts it in the game's rest pose
  ("low ready"): the grip at the right side of the waist, the stock by the
  ribs and the barrel forward and down, crossing to the left. An arm already
  posed is left alone.
  To adjust the grip use the weapon's Root control (yellow frame), don't
  move the weapon object: while connected, moving the object adds to the
  grip and pulls it out of the hand.
- Left hand: every weapon has the green "ST Grip" control, a ring under
  the barrel that slides along the weapon (only in that direction, along the
  green guide marking its travel) and sticks to the underside of the weapon.
  With the weapon connected and selected, "Connect left hand" switches the
  left arm to IK, puts the hand on the grip (palm on it, fingers along the
  barrel, thumb up) and hooks it on:
    * Sliding the grip (G) carries the hand along the weapon.
    * The left hand control still adjusts how it rests (move or turn it a
      little), without letting go of the weapon.
    * The "Left hand (grip)" switch lets go of it or takes it (animatable).
  If the grip is farther than the arm can reach, the hand falls short: move
  the grip closer to the handle or the weapon closer to the body.


11. KNOWN LIMITATIONS
---------------------

- The shadow is a fixed tint, not the game's color ramps.
- An .fbx exported with tools other than Blender may come with another
  scale or with the bone axes changed.
- Physics doesn't react to moving the root bone or to lifting the legs.


12. FOR DEVELOPERS
------------------

Modules (strinova_toon folder):
  importer.py   Import .uemodel / .fbx / skin folder and find the
                character's texture folder.
  shading.py    Toon materials, sun and head empties, lighting panel and
                outline.
  rig.py        Rig with Gacha Setup, fixes on top of it, and physics.
  face.py       Face sections, gaze, blink and face bones.
  paper.py      Paper version and the 3D / paper switch.
  accessory.py  Accessory bones and switches.
  weapon.py     Import weapons and props.
  common.py     Shared helpers.
  i18n.py       Spanish translation of the interface. UI text is written in
                English; a new UI string needs its line here.
  migrate.py    Renames what older versions created with Spanish names.
  materials.json  Data for every character and weapon material.
  __init__.py   Panel and registration.
